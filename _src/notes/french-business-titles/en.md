At a meeting in Côte d'Ivoire, the business cards you receive often carry a row of acronyms: PCA, DG, DAF… Treat them all as "manager" and you may end up negotiating, or signing, with the wrong person.

Côte d'Ivoire is a member of **OHADA**, the organisation that harmonises business law across much of francophone Africa. Its company law follows OHADA's Uniform Act, so the titles look much like France's, with some local habits.

## The titles at a glance

| Acronym | Full French title | Closest English | What to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PCA** | Président du conseil d'administration | Chair of the board | Chairs the board; may not run daily operations |
| **DG** | Directeur général | Managing director / CEO | Runs the business, reports to the board |
| **PDG** | Président-directeur général | Chair & CEO | When one person holds both PCA and DG roles |
| **DGA** | Directeur général adjoint | Deputy MD | Powers depend on what the company delegates |
| **DAF** | Directeur administratif et financier | CFO / Head of admin & finance | Budgets and payment terms often go through them |
| **DRH** | Directeur des ressources humaines | HR director | Hiring and expatriate staff |
| **DC** | Directeur commercial | Sales / commercial director | Often your first point of contact |
| **Gérant** | Gérant (of a SARL) | Managing partner | The legal manager of a SARL (limited liability company) |

## Three practical tips

**1. Before signing, confirm who represents the company.** A title on a card is not signing authority. For an important contract, politely ask for the company's registration extract (RCCM), which names its legal representatives.

**2. The PCA and the DG may be two people.** Board-run companies often separate the roles. Meet both if you can; if you meet only one, know who the other is.

**3. In writing, address the title, not the name.** Formal letters often open with *Monsieur le Directeur Général* or *Madame la Directrice*.

## One line for Asian teams

Translating a title correctly is respect; knowing who really decides is efficiency. Get both right and your first meeting is half won.
